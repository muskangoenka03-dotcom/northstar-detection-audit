Northstar Shop · How the system works

When a customer says “delete me,” is their data really gone?

Northstar marks deletion requests as done. But copies of a person’s data hide in many places: search, support tickets, analytics, backups. This system checks every request against the evidence and finds what was left behind.

An everyday way to think about it

Imagine you move house and ask the post office to forget you. They say “done.” But your name is still on the gym membership, the dentist’s files, an old storage box, and a friend’s address book that copies itself to the post office every night. This system is the detective who visits every one of those places and writes the to-do list to finish the job.

    The journey of one request

    Five steps, from “please delete me” to a fix list

    Read the evidence

    Database copies, exported files, system logs, PDFs and screenshots, each in its own format, are turned into one simple list of sightings.

    AI helps here

    Read the screenshots

    Claude AI looks at screenshots of admin screens and writes down what it sees. Each image is read only once and saved.

    Know every name they used

    People change emails and phone numbers. We gather their old and current details, and notice when an email later belonged to someone else.

    Follow the trail

    Starting from the person, we follow links to every record that points back to them, like pulling a thread.

    Decide and write the plan

    Is anything still there? If yes, list the fixes in a safe order. Either way, list the checks that prove it’s gone.

    Step 4, up close

    Following the trail: the identity web

    Each person sits at the centre. Around them are the details they’ve used. Around those are the records that mention them. If a record can be reached from the person, it belongs to their case. If a detail is shared with another person, the trail stops there so nobody else’s data gets pulled in by mistake.

    Why the order of fixes matters

    Turn off the tap before mopping the floor

    If you delete data while a sync is still copying it back in, it simply returns. So every fix plan follows the same three stages.

    First

    Stop the source

    Switch off syncs that would re-copy the data, and block backups from being restored.

    Then

    Clean it up

    Delete what shouldn’t exist, redact (black out) personal details in records that must stay, anonymise analytics.

    Finally

    Prove it

    Check each place again so there’s evidence the data is truly gone, cleaned, or legitimately kept.

    Rules it respects

    Not everything should be deleted

    Security logsKept for a set period by law, then they must go. The system knows the difference.
    Legal holdsRecords under a legal case are left alone until the hold is lifted.
    TombstonesA tiny “this account was deleted” marker may stay, but only with the ID number. No name, email or phone.
    Where AI is used, and where it isn’t

    AI only where rules can’t do the job

    Handled by clear rules

    • Reading files and logs. Same input, same answer, every time.
    • Deciding complete or incomplete. Fully explainable; no guessing.
    • Writing the fix plan. Follows Northstar’s deletion rules exactly.

    Handled by Claude AI

    • Reading screenshots. Rules can’t read pictures, so AI describes what’s on screen.
    • Explaining a verdict. A plain-English summary when someone clicks “Explain verdict.”
    • AI never changes a decision, and every answer is saved so it’s never paid for twice.

    How well it works

    Checked against known answers

    On 32 example cases where the correct answer was known, the system got the complete / incomplete decision right 32 out of 32 times. It matched the exact fix plan in 30 of 32. The two misses refer to records that don’t appear anywhere in the evidence provided. All 518 requests are checked in about 30 seconds.

    Jargon, translated

    Glossary

    Ghost data
    Personal data that still exists after a deletion was marked complete.
    Evidence
    The files each system left behind: database copies, logs, documents, screenshots.
    Sync pipeline
    An automatic connection that copies data from one system to another.
    Redact
    Black out the personal parts of a record while keeping the rest.
    Anonymise
    Keep the numbers, remove who they belong to.
    Tombstone
    A tiny marker that says an account was deleted, holding only its ID.
    Legal hold
    An instruction to keep records because of a legal matter.
